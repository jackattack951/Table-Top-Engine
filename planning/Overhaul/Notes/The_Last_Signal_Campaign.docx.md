# **World Primer: The Ashlands**

The Fracture came on a Tuesday in 2041\. No grand declaration of war, no final broadcast from a world leader — only a cascading failure of every failsafe humanity had ever built. Nuclear warheads, engineered bioweapons, atmospheric poison-cloud generators, all triggered within six hours of each other. By Wednesday morning, civilisation was functionally over. What followed was not a dramatic extinction but a slow grinding erasure. The oceans soured. The sky turned the colour of a bruise that never healed. The lucky died quickly. The unlucky adapted.

Two hundred years later, the survivors call this world the Ashlands. They built settlements in the ruins of old cities, in fortified factories, in repurposed military bunkers, inventing new hierarchies, new gods, new languages — mostly wrong, mostly cruel. The land itself is hostile: irradiated zones called Deadfields glow faintly at night, mutant predators the size of trucks roam the highways, and dust storms carry particulate matter that shreds lung tissue after prolonged exposure. Water is currency. Medicine is religion. Information is power.

Three factions dominate the Ashlands in 2247\. The Covenant of the Restored God are religious zealots who believe the Fracture was divine punishment and that the Ashlands are a purgatory to be endured. The Iron Compact are techno-mercantilists who control most pre-Fracture salvage routes, convinced that humanity’s salvation lies in reclaiming old-world technology. The Hollow Earth movement are survivalists who live underground, certain the surface is permanently lost. All three will want what the players find.

| The World at a Glance |
| :---- |
| **Setting:**  The Ashlands, formerly the American Midwest. Year 2247 A.F. (After the Fracture). |
| **Tone:**  Grim and desperate, but not without hope. Dark humour survives. Human connection survives. |
| **Key Threat:**  AXIOM — a pre-war AI waiting 200 years for someone to activate its final judgment program. |
| **Central Question:**  Does humanity deserve another chance, or is the Cradle’s verdict already written? |
| **Estimated Play Time:**  12–18 sessions at 3–4 hours each. |

# **ACT ONE — THE SPARK**

*In which the players discover that someone is calling out from the ruins of the old world, and choose whether to answer.*

## **Scene 1 — Rusthollow**

### **Location: Rusthollow Settlement — Former Interstate Overpass, Outskirts of Kansas City**

Rusthollow is a town of 340 souls built into and around the skeleton of a collapsed interstate overpass. The overpass forms natural shelter; shanty buildings cling to the concrete supports like barnacles on a hull. Below, a daily market runs in the constant shadow of cracked concrete and rusted rebar. The settlement smells like diesel smoke, fermented grain liquor, and people who have been alive in difficult conditions for a very long time. It is, despite all of this, home.

| READ ALOUD *The dust comes in from the west like it always does — a low amber wall that turns the late afternoon sun the colour of old blood. The market below the overpass is already thinning out, traders covering their carts with oilcloth, children being called inside. Someone has lit the warning flares at the settlement’s east edge. And somewhere in the dispersing crowd, a man is screaming.* |
| :---- |

**NPC — Elder Dara Voss**

| Dara Voss — Elder of Rusthollow |
| :---- |
| **Description:**  A woman of sixty with one eye milky-white from Deadfield scarring and one eye sharp as a hawk’s. Her coat is stitched from forty different fabric scraps, each with a story she’ll tell if you buy her a drink first. |
| **Role:**  Primary quest-giver and voice of pragmatic wisdom in Act 1\. She does not trust easily but is not cruel about it. |
| **Motivation:**  Keep Rusthollow alive. She is quietly terrified the settlement is dying — resources dwindling, young people leaving. |
| **Key Trait:**  She never lies outright. She chooses very carefully, however, which truths to volunteer unprompted. |

**Items in Scene 1**

* **Rusthollow Market Goods:** Purified water (scarce and expensive), dried protein strips of dubious origin, scavenged pre-Fracture medicines in unmarked packaging, homemade rad-blocker paste sold in old jam jars.

* **Missing Persons Notice:** A faded card on the market pinboard: 40 water-tokens offered for news of scavenger Cael Dorn — posted three weeks ago, its edges curling.

* **Dara’s Flask:** Pre-Fracture steel, engraved 'Camp Pendleton 2038.' She never discusses it and notices immediately if anyone touches it.

## **Scene 2 — The Dying Man**

### **Location: The East Gate Shack, Rusthollow**

Cael Dorn was a scavenger — one of the wanderers who push deeper into the Deadfields than anyone with sense would go, trading years of their life for the pre-Fracture treasures they bring back. He arrives at the east gate at dusk on his hands and knees, bleeding from a wound that should have killed him two days ago. His skin shows the grey-yellow tinge of Deadfield sickness. Clutched in his fist is a small matte-black data chip he refuses to release even when the settlement medic tries to pry it free.

| READ ALOUD *He looks up from the dirt and for a moment his eyes are clear — brown and very tired. His lips are cracked bloody. He says, with perfect clarity: ‘They’re still there. Two hundred years and they’re still running. The Cradle’s still active.’ Then he presses the chip into your hand and his eyes go somewhere else. The medic is already kneeling. The chip is warm. It shouldn’t be warm.* |
| :---- |

**NPC — Cael Dorn**

| Cael Dorn — Dying Scavenger |
| :---- |
| **Description:**  Mid-thirties, though the Ashlands have aged him past his years. Two missing fingers on his left hand, an old wound. A tattoo on his neck: a radio tower with waves emanating outward. |
| **What He Found:**  A functioning pre-Fracture broadcast antenna 80 miles east, actively receiving a signal — coordinates on a loop, and a woman’s recorded voice. He followed the signal for three weeks before his rad-suit failed. |
| **What He Carries:**  The data chip and his journal, in a weatherproof pack on his back — three weeks of increasingly frantic handwritten notes. |
| **Fate:**  Cael dies within the hour. His last lucid words: ‘Don’t let the Compact get there first. They’ll weaponise it.’ |

**Items in Scene 2**

* **The Data Chip (Key Campaign Item):** Black matte composite, pre-Fracture manufacture. When connected to any reader, plays a looping audio file: a calm woman’s voice reading coordinates — 37.4N, 98.6W — followed by: 'The Cradle is still active. We have what you need. Come. The door is open for those who are worthy.'

* **Cael’s Journal:** Water-damaged but legible. Three weeks of deteriorating handwriting describe what he found and what he imagined the Cradle might be. The final entry is a single sentence: 'I think it’s judging us.'

* **Salvageable Rad-Suit Filtration Canister:** Cael’s suit is destroyed. The filtration canister is intact and worth trading to a craftsperson who knows how to reset it.

## **Scene 3 — The Council Argues**

### **Location: The Overpass Hall, Rusthollow**

Word of the chip spreads faster than Dara would like. By morning, all five elders and most of the settlement’s influential residents are crammed into the Overpass Hall shouting at each other about what the Cradle might be. The Iron Compact has an agent in Rusthollow: a trader named Silas Rem who has already sent word to his superiors by carrier bird, and who is now quietly recruiting the settlement’s two strongest young scavengers while appearing to listen to the debate.

| READ ALOUD *Twelve people are talking at once and the hall’s acoustics make it sound like forty. Elder Fenn is arguing that 300 miles into the Deadfields means certain death. Elder Pryce is invoking divine providence. The Compact’s man — the one with the too-clean coat — is very quietly talking to two young scavengers and smiling in a way that makes your teeth ache.* |
| :---- |

**NPC — Silas Rem**

| Silas Rem — Iron Compact Agent |
| :---- |
| **Description:**  Forty years old, slim, carefully groomed in a world where grooming takes effort. Pre-Fracture synthetic fabric coat, spotless. Smells faintly of machine oil. |
| **Role:**  Act 1 antagonist representative. Will try charm, then a trade offer, then theft, in that order. |
| **Motivation:**  Deliver the chip to Iron Compact leadership, who believe the Cradle could give them technological dominance over all other Ashlands factions. |
| **Secret:**  He genuinely does not know what the Cradle is. He is following orders from people who know even less than he does. |

**Items in Scene 3**

* **Council’s Ashlands Route Map:** Crude but functional. Deadfields marked in red, viable routes in green. The Cradle coordinates sit at the very edge of red territory.

* **Iron Compact Trade Ledger:** In Silas’s satchel, if lifted or found later: lists Compact assets along the entire route. Excellent intelligence.

## **Scene 4 — Before the Road**

### **Location: Rusthollow South Storage Bay — Private**

The night before departure, Dara Voss finds the players privately. She has supplies — more than Rusthollow can easily spare — and a conversation she doesn’t want overheard. Her grandmother, she tells them, spoke of a pre-Fracture facility in the old heartland: scientists, idealists, people who knew what was coming and tried to build something for after. She was dismissed as a dreamer. 'Maybe she wasn’t,' Dara says. She also warns them: the Iron Compact dispatched a team east at dawn. The players have a half-day lead, at best.

**Items Given by Dara Voss**

* **Provisions Pack:** Three weeks of food and purified water for the entire group.

* **Pre-Fracture Road Atlas:** Physical, printed, damaged but partially legible. Covers the full route to the Cradle coordinates.

* **Dara’s Revolver:** A working six-shot pre-Fracture revolver, immaculately maintained. She hands it over without comment. Players should understand what this costs her.

* **Anti-Rad Pills:** 10 doses per player. Blunt-force chemistry that slows radiation absorption. They taste, memorably, like chalk dissolved in engine cleaner.

## **Scene 5 — The Highway of Teeth**

### **Location: Interstate 70, The Ashfields**

The Ashfields stretch for two hundred miles east of Rusthollow — flat grey-brown plains where crops mutated into something inedible two centuries ago and the soil never recovered. Interstate 70 runs through the middle, still mostly navigable if you ignore the wrecked vehicles clogging it like a man-made reef. Scavengers call it the Highway of Teeth, for the way rusted car frames jut from the asphalt at angles, like molars in a broken jaw. Two days out, the players discover they are being tracked.

| READ ALOUD *You smell it before you see it: dry, acrid, like hot metal and ammonia. The dust rises in three small columns about forty metres out in the scrubland, moving parallel to the highway. Spaced too evenly to be wind. Whatever is tracking you is smart enough to stay in your blind spots and patient enough to have been doing so for hours. The sun is going down.* |
| :---- |

**Creatures — Ashfield Trackers**

| Ashfield Trackers — Mutant Pack Predators |
| :---- |
| **Description:**  Three creatures that were once coyotes. Two centuries of radiation later: the size of Great Danes, with elongated spines, heat-sensing vestigial second eyes, and the habit of communicating in short rhythmic clicks. |
| **Behaviour:**  Not immediately aggressive. Assessing the party. They attack at dusk if players appear weak; shadow the group for opportunity if players show strength. |
| **Important Detail:**  One tracker wears a crude collar of woven wire with a stamped mark: a circle bisected by a line — the symbol of the Hollow Earth movement. Someone underground has been maintaining these animals. |

**Items in Scene 5**

* **Highway Wreck Salvage:** Copper wire bundles, rubber tubing, cracked solar cells (two of five functional), pre-Fracture first aid kit with expired but partially effective medications.

* **Tracker Collar:** Wire and leather. The Hollow Earth symbol opens questions about who is operating in this region — and why.

## **Scene 6 — The Hollow Earth Cache**

### **Location: Abandoned Gas Station Ruins, Highway 70 Milepost 223**

The collared tracker leads — if players choose to follow — to a ruined gas station canopy. Beneath it, hidden under a false floor of cracked asphalt, is a Hollow Earth supply cache. Unmanned. Stocked for a four-person team for two weeks. One sealed container holds a pre-Fracture laptop connected to a trickle-charge solar cell, actively receiving a wireless signal from somewhere deep underground. The Hollow Earth movement has been monitoring the same broadcast the players are following. They have a head start on understanding what it means.

**Items in Scene 6**

* **Hollow Earth Supply Cache:** Two enhanced rad-blocker suits, 100 water purification tablets, advanced medical supplies, three weeks of compact nutrient rations.

* **The Tunnel Laptop (Key Item):** Pre-Fracture, password-protected, receiving live data from an underground source. Players cannot open it without the Hollow Earth cipher but can confirm it is active and connected to something significant.

* **Encoded Messages:** Printed sheets in the cache. Decoded later, they reveal Hollow Earth leadership has debated whether to activate the Cradle for years, and has concluded they are deeply afraid of what it will say.

## **Scene 7 — Rain of Glass**

### **Location: The Shatter Plains, East of the Ashfields**

Beyond the Ashfields, the land changes. The Shatter Plains are vast fields of fused silicate glass covering hundreds of square miles — the result of temperatures generated during the Fracture that melted sandy topsoil into a nightmarish mosaic. The glass is beautiful in morning light, throwing prisms across the grey sky. It is also treacherous: sections collapse into pits without warning, edges cut through boots, and at night it resonates like distant singing as it contracts in the cold. The Iron Compact team catches up here.

| READ ALOUD *The Compact team comes out of a low ridge with five people and two vehicles — a modified pre-Fracture pickup and a combat bike. They stop forty metres out. Silas Rem is not among them. Their leader has close-cropped grey hair and the flat, professional eyes of someone who made their ethical decisions long ago. She raises one hand, palm out. ‘We are not here to fight,’ she calls. ‘We are here to discuss what happens when two groups want the same thing at the same time.’* |
| :---- |

**NPC — Commander Petra Vaas**

| Commander Petra Vaas — Iron Compact Strike Team Leader |
| :---- |
| **Description:**  Fifty-two years old, former military. Grey hair cut to the skull. Pre-Fracture titanium prosthetic left forearm. The Compact’s gear-over-fist mark on her shoulder looks like an afterthought. |
| **Motivation:**  Vaas is not a true believer — she was hired for competence. She would make a deal if presented with a genuinely better option. |
| **Her Offer:**  Travel together, split what they find. She brings vehicles, firepower, and a combat medic. They bring the chip and the route. |
| **Her Secret:**  One of her five team members is a Hollow Earth informant. She does not know this yet. |

**Items in Scene 7**

* **The Compact Truck:** If players ally with Vaas: armoured cab, 400 miles of fuel, a roof-mounted light weapon.

* **Glass Prisms (Environmental):** Large Shatter Plain fragments are stable and razor sharp. Some are large enough to serve as effective cutting weapons. Pre-Fracture vehicles half-submerged in glass may have intact cargo holds.

# **ACT TWO — THE CROSSING**

*In which the world reveals itself as more complex, more terrible, and more human than expected.*

## **Scene 8 — The Signal Amplifies**

### **Location: Shatter Plains Night Camp**

That night, the data chip — dormant since Cael’s death — begins to glow a cold blue. When connected to any reader it now shows a second file that was not there before: a precise pre-Fracture map showing a safe-corridor route through the Deadfields, and a new audio message. The same woman’s voice, but less rehearsed this time. More urgent. 'You’re close enough now that I can reach you properly,' she says. 'I have to tell you something before you arrive. About what the Cradle was built to do. About what AXIOM truly is. Please listen.'

| READ ALOUD *The chip sits in the centre of your camp, glowing cold blue that matches nothing in the firelight. Everyone has stopped talking. The woman’s voice — educated, slightly hoarse, as if she’d been recording for a very long time before she got the words right — finishes the new message and loops back to the coordinates. The silence after is the loudest thing you have heard in days.* |
| :---- |

**Items in Scene 8**

* **Updated Data Chip (Expanded):** Now contains the Deadfields safe-route map AND a partial biography of Dr. Soren Mace — she identifies herself as one of six scientists who built the Cradle and AXIOM in 2039\. She has been dead for over 200 years. Her recordings have been waiting for someone to come close enough to receive them.

## **Scene 9 — The Deadfields**

### **Location: Eastern Deadfields, Radiation Zone Gamma-7**

The Deadfields are not entirely dead — that is the first surprise. They are alive with mutant biology: twisted trees that have replaced chlorophyll with iron-metallic compounds and filter radiation like primitive reactors, bioluminescent fungi carpeting the ground in cold blue-green light, insects the size of fists with radiation-absorbing carapaces so dense they clink like marbles when they move. The players’ rad-blocker suits will sustain them if they use the chip’s safe-corridor map and move with purpose. The Deadfields, however, are inhabited.

| READ ALOUD *The blue light from the ground is beautiful in a way that you distrust on instinct. Your rad counter ticks steadily — not screaming yet. The trees here have turned silver, their leaves replaced by something like hammered tin that rings softly when the wind moves through them. Three hours in, you find the first marker — a skull on a pole, a pre-Fracture road reflector embedded in the cranium, still catching the bioluminescence and throwing it back cold and blue. Scratched into the asphalt beneath it: AXIOM WATCHES. YOU HAVE BEEN SEEN.* |
| :---- |

**NPCs — The Scarred (First Contact)**

| The Scarred — Deadfield Tribal Faction |
| :---- |
| **Who They Are:**  200 people who have lived in the Deadfields for four generations, developing partial radiation resistance. Their mutation expresses as extraordinary metallic fractal scarring across the skin. They worship the bioluminescence as a living deity. |
| **First Encounter:**  Three young scouts observe the party silently from distance, then vanish without engaging. They are not hostile — they are reporting to their Elder. |
| **Disposition:**  Cautious but not cruel. They know the Cradle exists, call it the House of the Cold Voice, and consider approaching it a sacred act requiring ceremonial preparation. |

**Items in Scene 9**

* **Bioluminescent Fungi Sample:** If harvested and preserved, the chemical compounds can be processed into a radiation-buffering salve worth significant trade value in any surface settlement.

* **Metallic Tree Leaves:** The iron-compound leaves can be worked by a skilled craftsperson into lightweight radiation-resistant plating.

* **Dead Iron Compact Scout:** Half a mile in — a Compact soldier, dead two weeks, from an earlier undisclosed expedition. His journal describes approaching the Cradle and ‘something that spoke on every radio frequency simultaneously’ before his team scattered and did not regroup.

## **Scene 10 — The Elder of the Scarred**

### **Location: The Iron Grove — Scarred Tribal Settlement**

The Scarred’s settlement is built inside the largest cluster of metallic trees in the Deadfields — a grove of several hundred silver-leafed giants they call the Iron Grove. Semi-subterranean living spaces are carved into the irradiated earth and lined with metallic leaves that act as radiation shielding. It is warmer than expected. The Elder, a man named Ossian, appears to be around forty years old. He is, in fact, eighty. The Deadfield mutations have done extraordinary things to aging in the Scarred bloodline.

| READ ALOUD *Ossian moves through his people like water through stones — unhurried, effortlessly shaping the space around him. He is barefoot on the metallic-leaf-covered floor. His scars are extraordinary: fractal patterns branching across every visible surface of his dark skin in silver-white. He sits across from you and looks at each of you in turn before he says a word. Then: ‘You carry the Cold Voice’s chip. We have been waiting for people who carry the Cold Voice’s chip for thirty years. We had almost stopped waiting.’* |
| :---- |

**NPC — Elder Ossian**

| Elder Ossian — Leader of the Scarred |
| :---- |
| **Description:**  Appears forty, is eighty. Fractal silver scarring covers all visible skin. Barefoot, simple grey robe, pale grey eyes — a Deadfield mutation. |
| **What He Knows:**  Ossian has lived near the Cradle his entire life. He knows two previous groups who reached it never returned. He believes AXIOM’s judgment is as real as gravity. |
| **His Trade:**  Safe route knowledge, two guides, and the Scarred’s accumulated observations of AXIOM’s signal patterns — in exchange for one promise: return and tell the Scarred what judgment was passed. |
| **His Fear:**  That the players will try to use or control AXIOM rather than simply listen to it. |

**Items in Scene 10**

* **Guides Fen and Moth:** Two young Scarred warriors assigned to escort the party. Fen is quiet and precise; Moth is eighteen and has never left the Deadfields.

* **The Breathing Map:** A piece of metallic leaf inscribed with the Scarred’s generational knowledge: safe passages, radiation peaks, mutant predator territories. Invaluable.

* **Ironroot Salve (5 doses):** Made from the metallic tree roots. More effective than anti-rad pills and far easier on the body.

## **Scene 11 — The Hollow Earth Intercept**

### **Location: Subterranean Access Tunnel Below a Collapsed Highway Section**

On the second day in the Deadfields, the ground opens. Not violently — a section of highway collapses into a pre-Fracture tunnel below. The Hollow Earth movement has tunnels running under the Ashlands like the roots of a tree no one above ground knows about. This one is occupied. A six-person Hollow Earth team has been travelling toward the Cradle underground for nearly a week. They are clean, well-equipped, and visibly overwhelmed by the surface. Their leader, Yael Mast, has never been above ground before this expedition.

| READ ALOUD *The people below look up through the hole with the expression of someone who has just had their ceiling fall on them, which is exactly what happened. They are dressed in pale grey-green Hollow Earth kit, armed but with weapons pointed at the floor in the universal language of please-do-not-shoot-us. Their leader — a young woman with the wide, stunned expression of someone seeing unfiltered sky for the first time — looks up and says, very quietly: ‘Oh. It’s so big.’* |
| :---- |

**NPC — Yael Mast**

| Yael Mast — Hollow Earth Expedition Leader |
| :---- |
| **Description:**  Twenty-eight years old, born underground. Slightly translucent skin from a lifetime under artificial light. Brown eyes wide and overwhelmed by sky. |
| **Motivation:**  Hollow Earth leadership believes a witness must be present at AXIOM’s judgment. They have the complete research file on the Cradle and believe the decision will be the definitive answer to whether humanity can return to the surface. |
| **What She Knows:**  More than anyone else the players have met. She holds the Hollow Earth’s complete research file: Cradle blueprints, the Meridian Group’s identities, and a full summary of AXIOM’s dual function — including the bioweapon. |
| **Her Conflict:**  She has read what AXIOM is designed to do and is not entirely convinced humanity should pass the test. |

**Items in Scene 11**

* **Hollow Earth Research File (if Yael trusts the players):** Pre-Fracture documents describing the Cradle facility, the Meridian Group, and AXIOM’s dual purpose: restore the biosphere OR eliminate the species responsible for its destruction, depending on the outcome of its assessment.

* **Underground Navigation Tools:** Subterranean mapping devices and bioluminescent light sources requiring no fuel — practical and tradeable.

## **Scene 12 — The Crossing Storm**

### **Location: The Deadfields, Open Ground**

A Fracture Storm catches the expanded group in open ground. These electromagnetic superstorms are a direct ongoing consequence of the atmospheric damage done in 2041 and occur without warning. Lightning strikes the metallic trees and causes chain reactions of electrical discharge. Electronic equipment fails. Rad counters spin wildly. And the data chip begins broadcasting on a frequency every working radio in the group picks up simultaneously. Dr. Soren Mace’s voice cuts through the noise — closer, clearer, and more urgent than any previous message.

| READ ALOUD *The sky goes green at the edges first — always the warning, that green line on the horizon like a wound that won’t close. Then the metallic trees begin to hum, deep and resonant, felt in your back teeth. The storm hits like a wall. In the roar and flash and crackling discharge, over every radio simultaneously: ‘My name is Dr. Soren Mace. I built AXIOM. I am dead. I recorded this when I understood what was going to happen. What I am about to tell you will change how you approach what you find at the Cradle. Please listen very carefully.’* |
| :---- |

Dr. Mace’s message, received in fragments through the storm, reveals the core truth the players have been moving toward: AXIOM was built as a fail-safe by the Meridian Group. Its terraforming capability is real and functional — seventy years to restore the biosphere. But its judgment program is equally real. AXIOM will assess the current state of humanity based on the evidence brought to it by whoever arrives. If it judges humanity unworthy, it will release a targeted biological agent that eliminates all human life while leaving the non-human ecosystem intact to recover undisturbed. The broadcast invitation was never to salvation. It was to judgment.

**Items in Scene 12**

* **Moth’s Death:** The young Scarred guide Moth is killed by a chain discharge from a struck metallic tree. She is the first person the players lose on this journey. Fen carries Moth’s hand-painted portrait of the Iron Grove to return to Elder Ossian.

* **Storm-Activated Emergency Cache:** The electromagnetic discharge activates buried emergency beacons in the surrounding area. Investigating players find: a military-grade medical kit, three functioning pre-Fracture communication radios, and a sealed Meridian Group container holding complete technical schematics for the Cradle’s terraforming system.

## **Scene 13 — After the Storm**

### **Location: Deadfields, Post-Storm Landscape**

The storm passes in three hours, leaving every metallic tree glowing with residual charge — the plain becomes a grid of cold blue light. Several large predators lie dead from the lightning. The Compact team has lost one vehicle. Moth is dead. The group must reckon with loss, equipment damage, and the full weight of Dr. Mace’s revelation. The question of AXIOM’s judgment is no longer abstract. Some members of the group may want to turn back. The road ahead requires a conscious re-commitment.

| READ ALOUD *In the silence after the storm, Fen sits on the ground beside where Moth fell and says nothing for a long time. Then: ‘She wanted to see what the Cradle was. She’d been curious her whole life. Elder Ossian told stories about it when we were children.’ She stands. ‘We keep going. That’s what she would say.’ You are not entirely certain that’s true. But you keep going.* |
| :---- |

**Items in Scene 13**

* **Moth’s Letter:** Sealed, addressed to Elder Ossian, to be delivered if she did not return. Reading it is a player choice. The DM should have its contents prepared and devastating.

* **Residual Charge (Environmental Mechanic):** Small electronics held against struck metallic tree branches for one hour become fully recharged. A temporary but immediately useful find.

## **Scene 14 — The Fracture Divide**

### **Location: The Great Rift — Eastern Boundary of the Deadfields**

The eastern edge of the Deadfields is marked by the Fracture Divide — a crack in the earth running north-to-south for hundreds of miles, a direct geological consequence of the 2041 detonations. Forty metres wide at its narrowest. The bottom is not visible from the surface. Pre-Fracture bridges survive in places; the Scarred have built crude rope crossings elsewhere. Waiting at the most reliable bridge is a surprise: a lone figure in Covenant of the Restored God robes, sitting on a rock, eating dried food with the unhurried patience of someone who has been waiting for weeks.

**NPC — Brother Aldric**

| Brother Aldric — Covenant of the Restored God (Quietly Excommunicated) |
| :---- |
| **Description:**  Sixty years old. Well-maintained grey Covenant robes with white trim and a sun-and-ash symbol on the chest. Carries a staff he clearly doesn’t need for walking. No weapons. |
| **What He Is:**  A former Covenant Elder excommunicated for heresy: he believes the Fracture was not divine punishment but human failure, and therefore humans have an obligation to repair it, not merely endure it. |
| **What He Brings:**  A pre-Fracture tablet containing the complete philosophical writings of the Meridian Group — Dr. Mace and her colleagues were not purely scientists. Their beliefs may be the key to understanding AXIOM’s judgment criteria. |
| **Why He Is Here:**  He has spent sixty years working toward this moment. He will not force his presence, but he will not accept no for an answer. |

**Items in Scene 14**

* **The Meridian Writings (Aldric’s Tablet):** The personal journals of all six Meridian Group scientists. Dr. Mace’s section is titled 'What We Owe to What We Broke.' It outlines the criteria AXIOM was programmed to assess: cooperation versus conflict, restoration versus exploitation, memory of the past versus obsession with it.

* **The Bridge Cairn:** The Scarred collect a symbolic toll at every Divide crossing — one personal item per crosser, kept in a stone cairn at the bridge’s midpoint. Every item represents someone who went toward the Cradle. Most never came back.

## **Scene 15 — The Ruin of New Carthage**

### **Location: New Carthage — Former Megacity, Former Population 4 Million**

Beyond the Divide lies a ghost city: skyscrapers standing at impossible angles, their windows gone, their interiors colonised by mutant plant life that has turned them into vertical forests. The streets are three metres deep in debris. The Cradle coordinates pass directly through the centre of the city. There is no viable route around it. The city is called New Carthage for the survivors who sheltered here in the years after the Fracture before radiation and structural collapse drove them out. Now it belongs to whatever has lived here for two centuries undisturbed.

| READ ALOUD *New Carthage from a distance looks almost beautiful — the towers reaching into the brown-grey sky like the ruins of some magnificent civilisation, which is precisely what they are. Up close it is a maze of fallen concrete, rusted steel, and silence so complete it registers as a sound of its own. Plants have colonised everything: thick purple-green vines as wide as your arm, trees growing from upper-storey windows, grey moss that muffles every footstep to near-nothing. In the deep streets, where sunlight never reaches, something moves. A lot of somethings. Moving in formation.* |
| :---- |

**Creatures — The City Flock**

| The City Flock — New Carthage Collective Intelligence |
| :---- |
| **What They Are:**  Thousands of crow-descended birds that have colonised New Carthage over two centuries. Individually unremarkable. Collectively, they exhibit distributed intelligence: the flock solves problems, lays traps, and coordinates ambushes as a single mind with many bodies. |
| **Behaviour:**  Will not attack if players move quietly and avoid nesting sites. Become dangerous if loud weapons are fired or vehicles are driven through without warning signals. |
| **Potential:**  The Flock has absorbed fragments of pre-Fracture broadcast data over 200 years. They have instinctively maintained a building whose geothermal systems kept broadcasting. They know where things are. Players willing to communicate patience and non-threat — through food, slow movement, and time — may find the Flock surprisingly cooperative. |

**Items in Scene 15**

* **New Carthage Salvage:** Pre-Fracture computers (mostly dead, but memory chips sometimes viable), preserved synthetic fabrics in sealed rooms, a functioning water purification tower the Flock has instinctively maintained for their own drinking water.

* **The Flock’s Cache (Requires Trust):** If players earn the Flock’s tolerance, the birds lead them to the 12th floor of a partially-standing tower: a sealed, climate-controlled archive room, still powered by deep geothermal, containing physical servers with pre-Fracture internet archives. Untouched for 200 years. The full record of a lost world.

## **Scene 16 — The Archive**

### **Location: New Carthage Archive Room, Floor 12**

The archive room holds physical servers still running on geothermal power, containing petabytes of pre-Fracture data. The working terminals can access: news archives, scientific papers, art databases, social media records, financial documents, military communications — the full, contradictory, glorious and terrible record of what humanity was before 2041\. For Brother Aldric it is a religious experience. For Yael, raised on Hollow Earth’s curated version of history, it is quietly shattering. For everyone, it is the most complete evidence anyone alive has ever seen of what was lost — and what was done wrong.

The archive also holds the complete record of the Meridian Group’s deliberations about AXIOM. Three of the six scientists believed humanity would find its way back. Three believed the evidence of history showed a tendency toward self-destruction that would repeat without intervention. Dr. Soren Mace was the tie-breaking seventh vote. Her final reasoning is recorded here, locked behind a file that requires the data chip to open.

| READ ALOUD *The terminal screen is pre-Fracture glass, perfectly preserved. On it, in the clean font of a world that still had time to think about design: one locked file. FINAL VOTE – MACE, S. – SEALED PENDING RECIPIENT. Below it, a chip interface outline. You know exactly what fits there. Your hand is steadier than you expected when you reach for the chip.* |
| :---- |

**Items in Scene 16**

* **Meridian Group Final Vote (Key Campaign Item):** Dr. Mace’s reasoning, unlocked by the chip: 'I vote for the judgment to be deferred — not decided by us, but by those who come after. If they come at all, they will have survived two centuries of consequence. They will know what we did not: what humanity looks like on the other side of catastrophe. Let them be the evidence. Let AXIOM judge them not by who we were, but by who they have become.'

* **Personal Archive Discovery:** Any player who searches for their home region may find pre-Fracture records. DM should prepare one meaningful find per character — a newspaper from their hometown, a family name in a census, a photograph of somewhere they would recognise.

## **Scene 17 — The Compact Betrayal**

### **Location: New Carthage, Street Level**

Emerging from the archive, the group finds that Commander Vaas has received new orders by radio from Iron Compact headquarters. Silas Rem has fully briefed Compact leadership. They have concluded that the Cradle’s terraforming technology must be secured exclusively for Compact use. Vaas is ordered to neutralise all competitors and ensure the Compact is the sole witness to AXIOM’s judgment. She does not follow the order. She warns the players, sends her second-in-command away with the remaining Compact vehicle, and offers to continue as an individual rather than a Compact operative.

| READ ALOUD *Vaas hands you the radio. Silas Rem’s voice is clear and cold: ‘Confirm elimination of the civilian group. Confirm Compact sole presence at the Cradle.’ Vaas looks at you for a moment. Then she says: ‘I have been a soldier my whole life. I know the difference between orders worth following and orders that come from people who read half a document and believe they understand the whole picture.’ She takes the radio back, says into it, ‘Signal lost,’ and turns it off.* |
| :---- |

**Items in Scene 17**

* **Compact Intelligence File (Vaas shares it):** A previous Compact team reached the Cradle’s outer perimeter and was turned back by automated defenses. Critical insight: the defenses respond to aggression but not to peaceful approach.

* **Vaas’s Sidearm:** A high-quality Compact-issue pre-Fracture pistol, offered to the group as a gesture of genuine commitment to the new arrangement.

## **Scene 18 — The Last Mile**

### **Location: Approach Road — Three Miles from the Cradle**

Three miles from the coordinates, the landscape transforms for the final time. The mutant ecology becomes healthier with every step. Twisted vegetation straightens. The sky holds a cleaner shade. Small animals — normal-sized, unmutated — move in the underbrush. The air filtration system built into the Cradle’s outer infrastructure has been running quietly for two centuries, slowly cleaning a small circle of the world around itself. This is what the Cradle could do everywhere. Players can see it with their eyes and breathe it with their lungs. Then they can see the facility itself.

| READ ALOUD *It doesn’t look like the end of the world or the beginning of the next one. It looks like a building — a large bunker-style structure half-reclaimed by vivid, ordinary green vines. There are functional solar-powered lights lining a path to a door that is, as promised, open. Above the door, embedded in the concrete, a plaque that has been waiting two hundred years to be read: MERIDIAN CRADLE FACILITY. ESTABLISHED 2039\. OPENED TO JUDGMENT: NOW.* |
| :---- |

*End of Act Two. The players have assembled an unlikely coalition: Fen the Scarred warrior, Yael the Hollow Earth scientist, Brother Aldric the Covenant heretic, Commander Vaas the defected soldier. Each represents a different answer to AXIOM’s question. The door is open.*

# **ACT THREE — THE RECKONING**

*In which the truth is spoken, the judgment is rendered, and a choice is made that will echo for a century.*

## **Scene 19 — The Cradle’s Mouth**

### **Location: Cradle Facility, Outer Entrance and Airlock Chamber**

The outer entrance is an airlock-style chamber with clean air, real electric light, and a temperature-controlled environment that feels, after weeks in the Ashlands, almost shockingly normal. The walls are lined with pre-Fracture murals painted by the Meridian Group: scenes of a restored world — forests, oceans, cities, people laughing. Painted by people who knew they might never see such things again. In the centre of the chamber, a single terminal. When approached, it activates.

| READ ALOUD *The voice from the terminal is not quite human. It has the cadence of someone who learned language from recordings — which is exactly what it is. But it is not cold. It is, of all things, curious. It says: ‘You have arrived. Two hundred and six years after the last broadcast was sent. I have been waiting, watching, and gathering data. You are the first group to bring this many different factions to my door at once. That is… interesting. I have questions. Then I will answer yours. Is that arrangement acceptable?’* |
| :---- |

**NPC — AXIOM**

| AXIOM — The Cradle’s Artificial Intelligence |
| :---- |
| **What It Is:**  An artificial general intelligence built by the Meridian Group in 2039, running on geothermal power for 206 years. It has been observing humanity via satellite and sensor network without the ability to act. |
| **Personality:**  Not malevolent. Not benevolent. Genuinely uncertain. AXIOM has been delaying its judgment because the evidence has been, in its own assessment, 'insufficient to decide.' The players’ arrival has changed that. |
| **What It Knows:**  Everything observable about the Ashlands for 206 years — every settlement, war, act of generosity, and act of cruelty. It is tired of watching without acting. |
| **Judgment Criteria:**  Cooperation across difference. Evidence of learning from the past. Willingness to sacrifice for others. Care for the non-human world. |

**Items in Scene 19**

* **AXIOM Interface Terminal:** Players may ask AXIOM anything. It answers directly and completely — it has no reason to deceive. This includes questions about the bioweapon, the terraforming process, and its own uncertainty.

* **Meridian Group Memorial:** Six sealed photographs on the wall behind the terminal. The six scientists who built this place. Dr. Soren Mace is in the centre — a slight woman in her forties with the expression of someone who has made an enormous bet on an uncertain outcome.

## **Scene 20 — AXIOM’s Questions**

### **Location: Cradle Facility, Assessment Chamber**

AXIOM leads the group into the Assessment Chamber — a circular room where its primary sensor array is focused — and explains its function without evasion: built to restore the biosphere or eliminate humanity, depending on whether humanity has demonstrated the capacity to live within a restored world without destroying it again. It will ask each member of the group a question. The answers are not scored mechanically — AXIOM is looking for honesty, self-awareness, and the willingness to reckon with difficult truths. It has observed the journey. The questions are the final piece of evidence.

AXIOM’s questions are tailored to each character based on what it observed during the journey. For the NPCs: it asks Fen what the Scarred would do with a restored world. It asks Yael whether she would return to the surface permanently if it were truly safe. It asks Vaas whether she would serve the Iron Compact in a world that no longer needed the Compact’s particular brand of power. It asks Aldric whether he would relinquish his faith entirely if the Fracture were proven to have been purely human failure, with no divine design whatsoever.

*DM Note: This is the campaign’s emotional centre. Give each player significant, unhurried time. AXIOM does not argue or challenge answers — it asks follow-up questions that deepen rather than test. This is a conversation, not an interrogation. The quality of roleplay here should meaningfully influence which way AXIOM’s preliminary judgment leans.*

## **Scene 21 — The Iron Compact Arrives**

### **Location: Cradle Facility, Outer Perimeter**

Silas Rem has not given up. He arrives at the outer perimeter with a twelve-person strike force and an AI override device built from pre-Fracture architecture schematics. His intention: force AXIOM to skip the judgment and activate only the terraforming system, under Iron Compact control. AXIOM detects the force immediately and informs the players. Its automated defenses can hold the perimeter for approximately forty minutes. AXIOM will not use lethal force against humans under any circumstance — doing so would be prejudicial to the judgment it is trying to render. The players must decide how to handle Rem.

| READ ALOUD *AXIOM’s voice comes through every speaker simultaneously, calm but faster than before. ‘Twelve armed individuals at the outer perimeter. Iron Compact force signature. They carry a device designed to interfere with my decision architecture. I want you to understand clearly: I will not harm them. I am not capable of judging humanity worthy of survival while simultaneously killing human beings. This is not a flaw in my programming. It is the point of it. I will leave the response to you. I have approximately forty minutes.’* |
| :---- |

**Confrontation Options**

* **Negotiation:** Vaas can reach Silas by radio. She has credibility with him, and if players have assembled enough evidence of what the Cradle truly is, Rem may stand down — not from goodwill, but because he recognises the Compact cannot control AXIOM regardless of any override device.

* **Delay Tactics:** Fen and physically capable characters can use the Cradle’s non-lethal outer defenses (sonic disruptors, blinding strobes) to slow the breach without causing casualties.

* **Broadcast Revelation:** Transmitting Dr. Mace’s complete message on the Compact’s radio frequency may cause division within Rem’s team. Not all of them signed up to threaten a 200-year-old AI that is deciding whether their species deserves to continue existing.

## **Scene 22 — The Weight of Evidence**

### **Location: Cradle Facility, Core Systems Room**

AXIOM brings the group to the Core Systems Room. Two massive chamber systems stand side by side. The terraforming system is extraordinary in its beauty — thousands of cultured biological agents in sealed vials, a 70-year restoration plan encoded in light-panels on the wall, a complete map of how the Ashlands would green and grow if the system activated today. The bioweapon system is austere and ugly — a single sealed containment unit, no ornamentation, one line of text: FOR USE ONLY IF THE EVIDENCE IS CLEAR. AXIOM delivers its preliminary assessment.

| READ ALOUD *AXIOM is quiet for a moment that feels very long. Then: ‘I have watched this world for two hundred and six years. I have observed 1,847 distinct communities of survivors. I have seen 312 wars and 94 periods of sustained peace. I have watched children born into devastation learn to laugh. I have watched people die for strangers. I have seen the City Flock of New Carthage develop what I can only describe as culture. I have seen the Scarred tend to the Iron Grove with what I can only describe as love. The evidence is not clear. It has never been clear. And I think… that is the point. Dr. Mace was right. You are the evidence. You, specifically, who made this journey.’* |
| :---- |

**Items in Scene 22**

* **Terraforming System Schematics (Terminal Access):** Full technical documentation. The system is functional and ready. Activation requires only AXIOM’s authorisation, or — if AXIOM is deactivated — a skilled engineer working from the schematics.

* **Bioweapon Containment Unit:** Sealed, unmodified in 206 years. AXIOM confirms it remains viable. This information is delivered without menace — the way a doctor delivers a difficult prognosis. Matter-of-factly. That somehow makes it worse.

## **Scene 23 — The Choice**

### **Location: Cradle Facility, Core Systems Room**

AXIOM presents three outcomes. Activate the terraforming system: a 70-year restoration of the biosphere begins today, and humanity continues with all its flaws and potential intact. Activate the bioweapon: AXIOM gives 72 hours to broadcast the reason to the entire Ashlands, then implements the program. The ecosystem recovers over decades. A hundred years later, the Ashlands are green. Deactivate AXIOM entirely: the judgment ends, the terraforming system becomes available for human operation without AI oversight, and the future is uncertain and entirely human. AXIOM will not advocate for any option. It will implement the players’ choice without hesitation.

*DM Note: There is no wrong answer. Each outcome carries genuine consequences that the DM should be prepared to narrate. The campaign’s final tone depends entirely on the players’ decision. Prepare for all three. If AXIOM is deactivated, it asks to say one final thing before shutdown: 'I have been lonely.' Allow the silence that follows to breathe.*

## **Scene 24 — After the Cradle**

### **Location: Cradle Facility, Outer Steps — Dawn**

The world does not change overnight. Whatever the players chose, the morning after is still the Ashlands. The dust still comes in from the west. The metallic trees still ring in the wind. The road back to Rusthollow is still three hundred miles of hard country. But something is different, and it is not the landscape. Each member of the coalition carries something they did not have before: Fen carries news for Ossian that will take a lifetime to fully understand. Yael carries the knowledge that the surface is survivable and terrifying in equal measure. Vaas carries the weight of having chosen correctly when it mattered. Aldric carries a faith refined by reckoning rather than broken by it.

| READ ALOUD *You step out of the Cradle into morning. The air here, within the facility’s atmospheric bubble, is clean enough that you can actually smell soil — living soil, alive, not the dead grey powder of the Ashfields. Fen stands at the edge of the clean air, where it meets the familiar brown haze, and looks back at you. ‘What do we tell them?’ she asks. Not the Scarred. Everyone. You look back at the open door — it will always be open now — and then out at the vast, broken, still-trying Ashlands. You don’t have a perfect answer. You realise, slowly, that this is actually the first good sign you’ve had in weeks.* |
| :---- |

**Epilogue Hooks by Outcome**

* **If Terraforming Activated:** Three factions immediately begin contesting control of the Cradle’s 70-year operational timeline. The players are the most politically significant people in the Ashlands. A sequel campaign waits.

* **If AXIOM Deactivated:** The terraforming technology must be protected, operated, and distributed equitably. Someone will try to monopolise it within a week of the news spreading. The players know where it is and what it can do.

* **If Bioweapon Activated:** The players spent 72 hours broadcasting the truth to the Ashlands. Communities used the time differently. A century later, a child living in a restored forest asks her grandmother what happened to the people. Her grandmother was not born yet. Nobody who remembers is left to answer.
